Your Duvet Isn't the Problem. Your Sheets Are.

Waking up too warm at three in the morning is usually blamed on the duvet. Most of the time the duvet is fine and the layer touching you is the problem.

The short version

  1. What sits against your skin decides how hot the bed feels. A lighter duvet over sheets that trap heat changes very little.
  2. Look for fibres that move moisture away rather than hold it. That is why linen and bamboo sleep cooler than the same weight of standard cotton.
  3. Choose oatmeal or a warm neutral over pure white. White photographs well and lives badly - every mark shows and it greys in the wash.

Why the bed feels hot even with a light duvet

Heat is only half of it. The other half is moisture. You lose water through the night, and if the layer against your skin holds that moisture, the bed feels warm and clammy even when the room is cool.

That is the part a lighter tog does not fix. Swap the sheets before you swap the duvet - it is cheaper and it is usually where the problem is.

Colour: why oatmeal beats white

Pure white looks right in a photograph and wrong in a room. It reads cool against a warm wall, so the bed sits apart from everything around it instead of belonging to the room.

It is also the least forgiving colour to live with. Warm neutrals hide the ordinary marks of a used bed and keep looking the same after twenty washes.

What to check before you buy

Fibre first, weight second. Linen, bamboo viscose and percale-weave cotton all breathe better than sateen at the same weight. Sateen feels smoother in the shop and warmer at 3am.

Weave. Percale is crisp and cool; sateen is soft and warm. Neither is better - they suit different sleepers, and the label will say which.

Depth of the fitted sheet. Measure your mattress including any topper. This is the measurement most people skip and the reason sheets pop off the corner.

Washing. Anything that needs a cold delicate cycle will not survive a real bedroom. Check it takes a normal wash before you commit.
