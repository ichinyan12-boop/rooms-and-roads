The Same Place, Two Hours Earlier, Is A Different Place

Avoiding crowds is usually treated as a question of where. Most of the time it is a question of when, and the fix costs one alarm rather than a longer trip or a different destination.

The short version

  1. Crowds arrive on a timetable. Hotel breakfast, coach tours and the first day-trip ferry all land people in the same square between about nine and eleven.
  2. There is an order that works: beach around six, viewpoint at first light, old town around seven, market at opening.
  3. The cost is an alarm and a nap after lunch. It is not a bigger budget and it is not a longer trip.

Why the crowd arrives at the same time every day

Almost nobody on a holiday chooses their own hour. Hotel breakfast runs from about eight to ten, coach tours are scheduled to arrive mid morning, and the first day-trip ferry or train is built around a return in the afternoon. Put those together and the busiest ninety minutes of the day are almost identical everywhere.

That predictability is the opportunity. Between first light and about nine the same street belongs to people who live there, and it is the version of the place the photographs were taken of.

The order that works

Beach, around six. The sea is flattest in the early morning before the day's wind builds, and the sand has not been walked over. This is the one people find hardest and rate highest afterwards.

Viewpoint, at first light. Not for sunrise specifically - for the twenty minutes after it, when the light is still low but you can see detail. Sunrise photographs are usually taken later than people assume.

Old town, around seven. Deliveries are happening and cafés are opening, which is more interesting than an empty film set and still nothing like the ten o'clock version.

Market, at opening. Markets are the exception to the empty rule: they are busy early and finished by lunch. Going at eleven means seeing what did not sell.

What to plan around

Nothing is open, including breakfast. Buy something the night before. This is the single most common reason people try it once and stop.

Check first light for your actual month. Seven in the morning in June and seven in October are not the same amount of daylight, and in autumn the early start is genuinely easier.

Timed-entry tickets do not care what time you got up. If the site has a booked slot, the early morning is for the streets around it, not for the site itself.

Plan the afternoon as nothing. The trade is real - an early start means a flat two hours after lunch, and pretending otherwise is how the second day gets abandoned.
