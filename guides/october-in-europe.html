In October The Crowd Leaves Before The Weather Does

October gets recommended as the cheap month, which is true and is also the least interesting thing about it. The reason to go is that two things move at different speeds: the crowd leaves on a calendar and the weather leaves on its own schedule, and the gap between them is the month.

The short version

  1. The sea holds summer heat for weeks after the air has lost it. Swimming in early October is often better than swimming in June, at the same air temperature.
  2. Autumn colour moves downhill faster than it moves south. Altitude shifts the date by weeks; latitude shifts it by days.
  3. The trade is what closes. Seasonal restaurants, ferries and mountain lifts shut for the year in October, and they do it on their own dates rather than the region's.

Warm sea, cool air, and why that order matters

Water changes temperature far more slowly than air does. A sea that has been warming since May is at close to its annual peak in September and is still comfortable well into October, by which point the afternoon air has dropped to the temperature people describe as pleasant rather than punishing.

In practice that means the two things people go to the coast for stop competing. You can walk at two in the afternoon without hiding from the sun, and still swim afterwards. In August you generally pick one.

Where the colour is, and when

Autumn colour is driven more by altitude than by how far north you are. A mountain valley turns two to three weeks before the plain below it at the same latitude, which is why 'when is autumn in Europe' has no useful answer and 'when does this valley turn' does.

The practical version: for early October look high - alpine valleys, the Dolomites, the Carpathians, Norway. For late October look low and south - central Italy, northern Spain, the French southwest. Booking the low country in early October is the common mistake, and it produces a green trip.

Weather matters more than the date at the end. A single windy week strips the colour early, so check a recent photograph rather than an average when you are inside a fortnight of the trip.

What closes, and how to check it

Ferries drop to a winter timetable. Island trips that run four times a day in August often run three times a week by late October. Look up the operator's own timetable page for your dates rather than a summary.

Seasonal restaurants and beach bars close for the year. Usually at the end of the local season rather than on a date you can predict, so check two or three specific places you would actually want to eat at.

Mountain lifts have a gap. Many close after the summer walking season and reopen for snow, which means late October can be the one month a cable car does not run at all.

The pricing is asymmetric. Going out cheap and coming back at the start of a half-term week is a common and expensive trap. Price the return leg first.

If you only do one thing
